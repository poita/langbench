# rrule performance benchmark

Regenerate with `python3 problems/rrule/bench/gen_bench.py` (fixed seeds). Expected outputs come from `reference/main.py`, which takes about 7 minutes in total, most of it on the year-9999 sweeps of `002`. When `.oracle-venv` exists at the repository root, the script also checks every line against python-dateutil. All three files have been checked, and dateutil agrees on every line. Set `NO_ORACLE=1` to skip that check. Run with `python3 run_tests.py rrule --tests-dir problems/rrule/bench -- <impl>/run.sh`. Every input stays within the SPEC limits: at most 5000 lines, `N`/`COUNT` at most 100000, `WEEKLY` rules ending through `N`/`COUNT`/`UNTIL`, and less than 5 MB of output per test. The output cap limits how much work a test can do by producing occurrences. Only rules that examine many candidate days per printed occurrence can push run time beyond about 0.1 s.

- `001_calendar_sync` has 5000 everyday calendar events, the kind a calendar server expands on a bulk sync: birthdays and anniversaries, fixed and n-th-weekday holidays, quarter ends, paydays, n-th weekday or first/last business day of the month, weekly and fortnightly meetings, weekday stand-ups, and daily routines. Each has a typical `COUNT`/`UNTIL` and `N` of 45-85, for 4.8 MB of output (298k occurrences). It measures per-occurrence throughput (period stepping, filtering and output formatting) at the largest output the spec allows. All four frequencies are mixed together, so no single fast path dominates. Because of the output cap it runs in only 0.07-0.3 s for the compiled implementations and about 1 s for Python.
- `002_sparse_sweeps` has 150 sparse but ordinary rules: Friday the 13th, a date falling on a given weekday, a leap day on a given weekday, week 53, the fifth weekday of February or of a 30-day month, day 366, and week 1 inside December. Each uses `YEARLY`, `MONTHLY` or `DAILY`, sometimes with `INTERVAL` 2-4, and `N` is 2000, 5000 or 100000. 136 of the 150 rules run to the year-9999 limit before reaching `N`, about 3.3 million candidate days each, which SPEC.md names as the hardest case. This is the algorithmically heavy part of the program: the cost of examining a period that yields nothing, including week numbering and ordinal-weekday computation.
- `003_mixed_workload` has 4990 rules in a realistic mix. 3400 random rules draw from the full value ranges (`BYSETPOS`, `BYWEEKNO`, `BYYEARDAY`, ordinals, any `WKST`) with small `N`. 1500 are everyday calendar events with `N`=20 and 80 are sparse sweeping rules with `N`=500. The remaining 10 are long expansions of 7k-100k occurrences: a daily run, a weekly run from 1800, last business day of the month, ordinal and week-number yearly rules, and leap-day and Friday-13th sweeps. The output is 4.4 MB. Every code path contributes a measurable share of the time.

## Long tier

`python3 bench/gen_bench.py --long` writes `bench-long/` (fixed seeds 19101-19304; `--only NAME` regenerates one input). Expected outputs come from `reference/main.py`: about 53 minutes in total on a heavily loaded machine (001 about 13 min, 002 about 18 min, 003 about 22 min), with 333 MB peak RSS. The C++, Rust, Go and D builds produce byte-identical output on all three inputs. The dateutil cross-check is skipped for the long tier. `--agree RUN_SH` (repeatable) takes the expected output from several implementations instead of the reference and requires them to agree byte for byte. It is not needed for the current inputs, because the reference fits its time budget. Run with `python3 run_tests.py rrule --tests-dir problems/rrule/bench-long -- <impl>/run.sh`.

- `001_calendar_sync`: 70,000 events (14x), N of 45-85 as before. Everyday events cost only about 0.1 µs per occurrence in the compiled builds, so output alone could not reach the time target without about 1 GB of output. 65% of the events (45,555) therefore come from `seasonal_event`, which produces calendar events confined to part of the year: n-th/last-weekday and fixed-date public holidays and closure days written as `DAILY` rules with `BYMONTH`/`BYMONTHDAY`/`BYDAY` filters, every-Saturday-in-summer and weekday summer-camp routines, seasonal `WEEKLY` meetings, the first or last business day of the year (`YEARLY` + `BYSETPOS`), and fiscal-week `BYWEEKNO` dates. The other 24,445 events come from the unchanged `calendar_event`. Most of the run time now goes into scanning filtered-out off-season days, not into printing. Output is 65 MB (4.09M occurrences).
- `002_sparse_sweeps`: 1000 rules (6.7x), with the same generator and fresh seed 19201. 338 rules have N=100000 and nearly all of them sweep to year 9999. Output is 20 MB.
- `003_mixed_workload`: every group is 15x larger with fresh seeds: 51,000 wide-value random rules, 22,500 calendar events with N=20, 1200 sparse rules with N=500, and 150 long expansions. The long expansions are randomized variations of the ten fixed ones (same frequency, filters and N, with a different start, time and weekdays). The input has 74,850 rules and 61 MB of output.

Measured on a heavily loaded 8-core M-series Mac. CPU user time is the reliable figure, and wall time was within 2% of it on these runs:

| input | fastest (user / wall) | Rust | C++ | Go | peak RSS (max impl) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 001 | D 7.2 s / 7.3 s | 7.4 s | 9.2 s | 10.5 s | 155 MB (Go) |
| 002 | D 7.8 s / 7.9 s | 7.9 s | 9.8 s | 11.7 s | 55 MB |
| 003 | D 7.5 s / 7.5 s | 7.6 s | 9.4 s | 11.3 s | 156 MB (Go) |

The Python build passes all three inputs: 001 in 335 s user (351 s wall), 002 in 417 s (451 s) and 003 in 373 s (389 s), with at most 147 MB peak RSS. Every run is under 10 minutes. All five builds pass. Go exceeds the 10-second per-test timeout that SPEC.md suggests (10.5-11.7 s user). `run_tests.py` sets no timeout by default.

`bench-long/` is 149 MB on disk, mostly output. This exceeds the 100 MB target because 001 and 003 scale by output-producing rules. SPEC.md limits raised for the long tier only (`bench-long/`): up to 100,000 input lines (was 5000) and less than 100 MB of output per test (was 5 MB). N, COUNT, value ranges, the `WEEKLY` termination guarantee and all semantics are unchanged.
